# Choose a strategy with a purpose

Reconstructed draft — review before submission.

## Learning target

After a 30-minute activity, learners classify at least six of eight component cards as input, processing, output, or storage, and explain one complete information-processing path.

## Selected strategy

Cooperative card sorting with a short demonstration and peer explanation. Begin with a familiar action, model one classification, and let groups sort and justify the remaining cards.

## Learner participation

Minutes 0–5: demonstrate the input–process–output–storage path. Minutes 5–15: groups sort cards with rotating facilitator, reader, sorter, and reporter roles. Minutes 15–23: explain disagreements and revise. Minutes 23–30: complete an individual exit task.

## Why this strategy supports the target

Sorting directly practices classification; explaining choices reveals reasoning; the individual exit task checks understanding beyond group performance. A diagram and spoken explanation offer complementary ways to follow the process.

## Make it more inclusive

Provide large-print, high-contrast cards with both names and pictures. Offer digital or paper versions and permit pointing, typing, or speaking. Use a word bank, extra processing time, and roles that do not depend on speed.

## Three provisional checks

Outcome fit: the task practices the target classification. Learner fit: scaffolds support beginners and multiple participation modes. Feasibility: printed cards work without internet in 30 minutes. These are proposed checks, not a transcription of the course’s three filters.
