My take on homework: 
	
For me I do not think homework should be all or nothing. Homework often works 
best for students who have quite and learner conducive environments or homes. And it 
can make homework very tedious. A strict no homework policy also has its cons 
because some skills might only stick or flourish if constant practice is being done for 
example like programming you wont get better by reading forums or digital material you 
learn by doing it. So Im in between less homework, but better homework.  
If Im to be given and assignment it needs to correlate with the lesson being taught to us 
because what good is homework if the homework given does not correlate with the 
lessons being taught by the teacher. Or if its a project id make sure it is feasible and 
doable by my students in terms of time, resources and man power. Because if Im just 
gonna cram my students with homework or projects that do not benefit them then I have 
failed to educate them as an educator. Learning should be challenging but not torture. 
Learning is best when learners are positive and motivated. 
3 Question Techniques: 
- Give ample wait time. If I ask a question I shall wait at least 5 to 10 seconds before 
calling anyone so I can give my students time to think or focus on the questions. 
- Always give a follow up question. This allows assurance the learner has learned 
something. This also gives them to opportunity to show what they’ve learned and how 
much of it they understand 
- Asking open ended questions. Instead of only asking recall questions, I’ll ask things 
like “Why might this approach fail?” or “How could you improve this?” Students think 
on their own first, then talk with a partner, then share with the class.
