For me I believe assessment is not the end of learning but part of it. Its main 
purpose is to show learners where they are, where they need to go, and how to get 
there. A score alone cannot do that. Assessment should therefore help learners 
improve by giving them clear information they can act on, not just a grade to accept. 
For assessment to help learners improve, it must come with timely, specific 
feedback. Comments such as "good job" or “needs work” teach very little, while 
feedback like “your logic is sound, but your loop does not handle an empty list” tells 
a learner exactly what to fix. I would use formative assessments such as short 
quizzes, exit tickets, practice tasks, and draft submissions, and give learners the 
chance to revise their work. Mistakes become the starting point for learning instead 
of the final verdict. 
I believe fair, valid, and transparent assessment rests on three commitments. It is 
fair when every learner has an equal chance to show what they know, which means 
considering different learning needs, offering varied formats where appropriate, and 
avoiding trick questions. It is valid when it measures what was actually taught: if I 
teach problem-solving, I should not grade memorization. It is transparent when 
learners know the expectations before they begin.
