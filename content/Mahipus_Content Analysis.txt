Part 1: Concept Check 
Content selection criteria are the standards used to decide what belongs in a lesson. 
The common ones are: 
•
Validity: content actually aligns with the stated objectives. 
•
•
Significance: it contributes something meaningful/transferable, not trivia. 
•
•
Learnability: it’s appropriate to students' developmental level and prior 
knowledge. 
•
•
Feasibility: it can realistically be taught in the time/resources available. 
•
•
Interest: it’s relevant to learners' needs or future use. 
•
•
Simple to complex: basics before advanced application. 
•
•
Prerequisite learning: skill A must come before skill B if B depends on A. 
•
•
Chronological: order of events in time. 
•
Known to unknown: build on what students already know. 
A sequence is flawed when it violates prerequisite learning asks students to do 
something before they've learned how or when assessment/application happens before 
instruction. 

Part 2: Application 
Problems in the outline; 
1. Monday: Had a wrong sequence since it violates prerequisite learning students are 
asked to rewrite and defend a curriculum outline on Day 1, before they've been taught 
what content selection criteria or sequencing patterns even are. This is the most 
advanced task in the whole week, and its placed first. It's like asking students to edit an 
essay before teaching them grammar. 
2. Wednesday: Had the wrong sequence because they did an advanced task too early 
an advanced critique activity involving a multi subject curriculum is scheduled on Day 3, 
right after only one day Tuesday of introductory content. Critique of a complex case is a 
higher-order skill that should come after students have practiced on simpler examples 
not right after the first exposure to the concept. 
3. Thursday: misplaced assessment 
A 10 item quiz on content selection criteria is given on Thursday, but content selection 
criteria isn't actually taught until Friday's lecture. Students are tested on material before 
it's presented this violates basic instructional logic assessment should follow instruction, 
not precede it. 
4. Friday: A foundational content placed last 
The lecture on basic concepts of content selection is the most foundational, introductory 
content in the whole unit, yet it's taught last. Foundational content should anchor the 
sequence at the start, since sequencing Tuesday and critique Wednesday both depend 
on understanding selection criteria first. 
5. Overall outcome misalignment 
Because of the ordering, several outcomes are unreachable when scheduled Monday's 
outcome justify their revised outline requires knowledge students don't have yet; 
Thursday's outcome recall important concepts tests unlearned concepts.
